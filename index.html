Before an agent can help a team, it needs to understand
what its members know, depend on, and still need to resolve.

The task

From group history to collaboration context

Agents entering an ongoing collaboration need context about how the team thinks and works: which beliefs participants have expressed, which responsibilities have been accepted, and where coordination may break down. We introduce group briefing, the task of reconstructing this task-relevant group cognition from a collaboration request and the history available at a specified cutoff.

Our benchmark evaluates both the information a brief preserves and its use in downstream collaboration. Across memory methods and nine writer models, a persistent challenge emerges: recovering past state is easier than identifying future friction. We introduce GroupBrief, a task-guided retrieval and synthesis procedure, and then study how coverage feedback can improve its policy while keeping the writer fixed.

The method

Build a brief. Give the agent context.

GroupBrief retrieves evidence for the request, then connects it into an attributed account of group cognition.

01

Theory of mind

Preserve participants’ expressed beliefs and assumptions, including who holds them and what remains uncertain.

02

Group dynamics

Connect roles, responsibilities, dependencies, and tensions around the team’s shared work.

03

Grounding gaps

Keep unconfirmed acknowledgment or alignment visible, so an agent can decide what needs clarification.

A closer look

What does the optimized policy learn to preserve?

A healthcare case illustrates how more explicit search and writing instructions change the coordination context in the brief.

01

Follow the decision

Read replies and later pre-cutoff updates about the same work item.

02

Separate roles and conditions

Distinguish who confirms an arrangement from who owns it, and retain when that responsibility ends.

03

Preserve the purpose

Connect an arrangement to the specific coordination problem it is intended to prevent.

This post-hoc case illustrates revised instructions and observed brief differences. It is not an executed tool trace or a causal demonstration of improved downstream collaboration.
